Not a trade body speaking for the industry from the outside. AFCA's working groups are staffed by engineers and policy leads who are already installing chargers, negotiating tariffs, and arguing with grid operators this week.

Who we are

AFCA formed because Africa's EV charging landscape was growing faster than the coordination around it. Left alone, that growth produces forty different plug standards, forty different payment apps, and a driver who has to carry four adapters and three logins just to get across a border. We bring charge point operators, automakers, utilities, standards bodies, and government agencies into the same room to solve interoperability, financing, and grid readiness before those problems get baked into concrete and copper.

What we do

Set Shared Standards

We coordinate on open protocols so hardware from one manufacturer and software from another actually work together, across networks and across borders.

Advocate for Policy

We engage regulators and ministries directly, before rules are finalized, to align incentives, tariffs, and permitting with a market that's moving faster than most regulatory calendars.

Build Capacity

We run working sessions where operators and technicians trade what's actually working, not conference-stage highlight reels.

Track Progress

We publish data on charging infrastructure growth across African markets — the kind investors and ministries can use to make real decisions, not just cite.

Our values

Openness

Interoperable systems over walled gardens, even when a walled garden is the easier product to build.

Equity

Reliable charging access for every market we work in, not just the capital cities that get funded first.

Collaboration

A shared problem gets a shared solution — one built with the operators who'll have to live with it, not handed down to them.
